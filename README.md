# MYRIA — a drop of living light

A soft-body game about a formless creature of luminous hydrogel that lives between the glass plates
of a microscope slide. It reaches with a crown of feelers, drinks light, squeezes through gaps, is cut
into siblings and fuses whole again — and it grows.

**Play:** open `index.html` in a browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari). It is one self-contained
file: no server, no install, no network. Any window shape works, portrait included.

## Controls — one stick's worth

| Intent | Mouse | Keyboard | Touch | Pad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flow** | move the pointer — the creature follows it; rest the pointer on it and it rests | WASD / arrows | drag anywhere (an anchor wakes under your thumb) | left stick |
| **Dart** (a burst of speed; costs a little mass you can fetch back) | click | Space | tap, or flick | A |
| **Lance** (a coiled dart: it cleaves a bigger neighbour in two) | hold, then release | hold Space, release | a hard flick | hold A, release |
| **Bud** (throw off a sibling) | right-click | E | second-finger tap | X |
| **Swell / pop** (hold a breath: two-thirds more silhouette, the same mass; let go to shove) | hold right button | hold F | hold a second finger | Y |
| **Rally** (call siblings home to fuse) | hold middle button | hold Q | pinch | B |
| **Focus** another sibling | click it | Tab | tap it | bumpers |
| Pause | ‖ | Esc / P | ‖ | Start |

The pause menu can also **keep a picture** — the frame on the glass, saved as a PNG — and holds two
comfort switches: **Tilt** (phones and tablets: the pose you switch it on in is "level"; tip the
device and the creature runs downhill — the thumb-stick still overrides it) and **Sharp /
Smooth** (sharp keeps the picture crisp and paces to thirty frames when a machine cannot afford
sixty; smooth makes the picture softer instead, to hold sixty wherever the hardware can).

Nothing else to learn: feelers fetch nearby light by themselves, pushing into a gap softens the gel
so it flows through, resting near a pad or an open pore settles you onto it, and resting against a
wall where something is watching makes you a veil it cannot see.

## What is in it

- **The Atrium** — the title is written in motes. Wake the sleeping pearl and eat the word. A brand-new
  save meets nothing else but one door (the way in) and a halo of dew round the sleeper, so that a first
  unknowing nudge in any direction is answered with light inside a second; the other doors arrive, all
  at once, when the first chamber has been lit. Each door wears its name; only the one you are beside
  says more. The living light of every place you light follows you home: the first chamber sends
  fireflies, the Dew a school that circles the word, the Glassworks seed pods on the ceiling (they
  regrow), Chemistry shy sparks and drifting light, the Heart a golden swarm. The pause menu there
  keeps an **Album**: the game takes a small picture by itself at the moments that matter — a trick
  found for the first time, a warden passed, a new size, a Bloom medal, a haunting laid to rest, the
  last room — each with a line under it, newest first.
- **Odyssey** — twenty-eight single-screen chambers in four strata (Dew · Glassworks · Chemistry ·
  Abyss), each teaching one idea through physics alone: squeezing, darting through a soap film,
  budding to hold two pads, threading a comb, blades, sling bands that throw you whole through doubled
  films no dart can break (and one flat, pale band that aims nothing: it gives back the mirror image of
  whatever strikes it — the vault under the lace only opens to a bank shot), grinders and healing springs, pistons, sticky resin against a river,
  quicksilver among hunters, an effervescent foam whose every dart is a free jet that skims a river
  too strong to wade (wade it anyway: a one-way valve turns being swept off into a flume ride home),
  membranes that stop the whole of you and let any part of you through — send a piece where you cannot
  go, hold two switches on two sides of a door that only opens one way —
  oil that will not mix, shear-thickening armour, ferrofluid leaping between pulsing poles, frost that
  stiffens you and vents that thaw a frozen door, watchers you hide from by holding still, a gyre of
  currents, a black hole, bluffing giants with a held breath, a spore bath that turns whatever you
  leave behind into a ripening decoy — and whatever swallows it into a rain of dew — a hunt, and a
  heart that needs you in three places at once. Four **wardens** close the strata: **The Lock**
  (two rings of glass turning against each other around a heart of light — every doorway is a moment,
  not a place), **The Centrifuge** (a drum whose three arms sweep you past lanterns and gears: you do
  not cross it, you ride it) and **Twin Cores** (two electromagnets circling one another inside a ring
  of gears: as iron you are drawn in, the pole flickers, leans, and throws you — to its twin, unless
  you lean elsewhere; coat both long enough and the pair falls together and pays out) and **The Iris**
  (two rings of glass louvres round a heart of light that breathe in turn, eight beats of its own
  music to a breath — the outer ring first, the inner a half-breath later: turned across, every gap is
  a doorway; laid flat, a wall; a beat before they move, the blades flash. A ring closing on you leans
  on you rather than slicing — the gel is pushed aside — and for a moment as one ring closes and the
  other opens, a straight line runs to the heart. In on one breath, out on another). Three living
  stars per chamber: **Radiance** (all the light), **Grace** (lose almost nothing), **Swift** (under
  par). The pore opens at 60 % — the rest is appetite. Between one chamber and the next the result
  card carries a **long exposure** of the run: the chamber darkened like a plate left open, and the way
  you went drawn across it in light — cool where you woke, warm where you went home, brightest where
  you lingered — every light you took a spark, every one you left a dim ring, the echo you raced a
  pale thread (a warden passed, or a chamber of your own sealed, keeps that picture in the Album).
  Twenty-five named tricks (Thread the Needle,
  Surgical Shear, Ghost Wall, Paper Tiger, Cleave, Slingshot, Gravity Assist, Hole in One, Bank Shot,
  Go-Between, Trojan Swallow, Outrun, Comet, Phoenix, Immaculate, Net, Velvet, In Time, Harvest, Whole, Maker,
  Neighbourly, Unseen, Garden, Lighthouse) are noticed when you find them; the Atrium keeps a
  journal in which the ones you have not found yet are riddles. The stars you gather dye you: six
  **hues** (Coral · Honey · Rose · Jade · Pearl · Nebula) unlock as they add up — each a full optical
  recipe, chosen from the pause menu in the Atrium.
- **Echo** — clear a chamber and your best run stays in it: come back, and a pale bead of your own hue
  retraces that run beside you, to race. The pause menu can **copy your echo** as a short code; anyone
  who pastes it into *Race an echo* in their Atrium races your bead (in blue) through that chamber —
  two players, no server, no clock in common.
- **Myriad** — the last room has no door. Everything you met on the way is there, and nothing in it is
  hungry; what you do with all that light is the ending. (Afterwards the Atrium keeps the morning.)
- **Living light** — not every light waits to be eaten. **Schools** swim their loops as one body
  (cohesion, alignment, a startle that runs through all of them) and scatter from anything fast —
  hold still and they come to look at you. **Shy sparks** read how fast you are closing on them and
  bolt; drift in slowly and they never know. **Fireflies** blink on their own clocks and, coupled to
  their neighbours, fall into one flash over a few seconds — a firefly between flashes is an ember a
  feeler will not reach for. Where music is playing, a cloud slowly finds *its* pulse too, and
  flashes on the downbeat — and a creature resting beside it shimmers with the flash. At rest, the
  creature turns a feeler to watch whatever moves nearby. **Drift** rides the currents. **Seed pods** burst when brushed, flinging
  their seeds. Each has its trick: four of a school inside one heartbeat (*Net*), a shy spark that
  never knew you were there (*Velvet*), three fireflies in one flash (*In Time*), every seed of a pod
  before it settles (*Harvest*).
- **Flow** — light taken in quick succession becomes a chain. A chain makes the crown *bloom*: longer,
  brighter feelers that fetch from further, a faint pull on nearby motes, a little more speed, a
  rising melody — and every eighth link pays a bonus of mass. Greed with rhythm is rewarded; nothing
  is punished.
- **Bloom** — seventy-five seconds of rain, and the garden it leaves. Light falls in figures — a
  line, a wave, an arc, a ring, a spiral, a cross, a flower — each its own colour, its stars strung on
  a faint thread. Every star scores; take *every* star of a figure before one fades and the figure is
  **drawn**: it rings out (a little run that climbs with the multiplier), flares, and is etched into
  the dish for good. Drawn figures are the multiplier's steps — ×2 after one, ×8 only after fourteen in
  a single unbroken chain (pips beside the ×N show how far to the next). The chain is what holds the
  steps up: leave it hungry and a fuse burns round the creature until it falls quiet; touch the blade
  that wakes at eighteen seconds (or the gear at thirty-eight) and it is cut — either way, back to ×1.
  The last twelve seconds are a downpour, every figure counting twice. The creature blooms to a nimble
  size and no further (a giant would sweep the dish, and could not slip past a blade). Visitors still
  come — a school that scatters, fireflies taken only while lit — but they are bridges, not figures.
  The run ends on its garden: every figure drawn, in its colour, grown again on the card, the figures
  tallied as glyphs (╱ ∿ ◠ ◉ @ ✚ ✿ — the same line goes into *Copy result*). Draw all seven kinds in one
  shower for the trick **Garden**. Four medals: Dew · Pearl · Bloom · Prism.
- **Vigil** — the dish is dying, and you are the last light in it. Your light burns down a grain at a
  time (a little faster with every omen seen, and faster the brighter you burn), so it has to be fed —
  and while an omen rages, light falls only where that omen is worst. Six omens, each announced a
  breath before it lands (its name, a line of warning, its colour breathing at the rim): **The
  Drought** (the pools shrink and the floor cracks; the light falls in one pool at a time, the next
  glimmering first, and dry ground steams you away), **The Frost** (the cold creeps in from the rim and
  the one warm spring wanders; the light lies out in the cold, and a body frozen through flakes until a
  dart cracks the ice), **Glass Rain** (a red seam shows each shard's path; the glass follows it edge-on
  and leaves light in its wake), **The Quake** (chevrons show the way the ground will heave — lean
  against them; every jolt shakes light loose at the rim it throws you toward, where gears grind),
  **The Maelstrom** (the water turns round a dark heart that eats whatever touches it; light spirals
  in, richer the nearer it gets) and **The Eclipse** (the dish goes dark but for your own glow; eyes
  sweep from the rim and burn whatever moves in their sight — be still against the wall and they pass
  over you). A lull of light follows each; after the first six they come in pairs. At ten grains you
  are an ember, and an ember that eats nothing for eight seconds goes out. The run ends on the long
  exposure of where you kept your light and the story of every omen you saw through (◌✓ ❄✓ ╲✗ — the
  same line goes into *Copy result*). See all six through in one vigil for the trick **Lighthouse**.
  Four medals for how long the light lasted: Spark · Lantern · Beacon · Sun.
- **Abyss** — an endless generated descent with grazers, lancers, thornbacks and oil for neighbours,
  each measured against your own size; from the fourth depth on one of them is an apex that only wit
  (a lance, a held breath) will answer. Vaults — rings of glass turning around a hoard — are worth the
  wait at their doorway; deeper down there are turnstiles, sling bands, lodestones (an iron bath
  beside a pulsing pole crowned with light), **geysers** (white holes: they draw breath, then throw
  whatever stands on them — and a little light — outward), **sponges** (desiccant: thirsty pores drink
  whatever leans on them, a grain at a time, until they are sated — strike one with a dart and it is
  wrung out, everything it ever drank bursting free for whoever is nearest) and **mimics** (something
  curled up wearing a ring of light: its lights blink all together, there is a knot of thorns at its
  heart, and no feeler ever reaches for it; wander in and it bites, dart through it and it bursts into
  the light it pretended to be). Its rooms have fauna of living light too. The daily dive's rule mutation may now be *White Tide* (a geyser in
  every chamber) or *Masquerade*. Bigger membranes siphon smaller ones (the swallowed pigment swirls
  inside you before it becomes yours). Every second chamber you choose one of three ways to change;
  every fifth is a predator dish. Three embers.
- **Hauntings** — where a dive ends it leaves something of the diver: a tacky stain, false lights where
  it bled, and — for whoever comes that deep later and steps in the stain — a pale **revenant** of the
  body it was, in the ghost of its hue, with a small name of its own. It rises as your equal: outgrow it
  and swallow it, or cleave it, and it is laid to rest (its light comes back to you, and it will not be
  waiting again). "Whoever" is you on a later dive — or a stranger: the end of a dive offers *Copy my
  haunting*, one line of text; pasted into *Invite a haunting* in anyone's Atrium, your remains wait
  for them at that depth. Asynchronous, serverless, and a little personal.
- **Daily** — the same for everyone, from the date alone. **The day's dive** is bent by one of fifteen
  mutations of the world (Thick Water, Hungry Dark, Feast, Glass Storm, White Tide, Masquerade, Quick
  Tide, Glass Water, Long Winter, Rapids, Slingshot, Famine, Giants, Champagne, Firefly Night) — two at once on
  weekends, never a pair that cancels out. **The day's sprint** is one Odyssey chamber in mutated water
  (faster, thicker, frictionless, or "every light or no door"), against the clock, drawn only from the
  100 chamber × mutation pairs the autopilot finishes in both orientations. **The day's rain** is a
  Bloom whose figures fall in the same order and in the same places for everyone on a screen of the
  same shape (the rain never
  leans toward where you happen to be, and every draw of its dice is taken whatever you do — only how
  fast the figures come still answers how fast you eat). *Copy today* makes a result line to compare
  with anyone's.
- **Duet** — two players, one screen: pointer + W A S D / Space / F on a computer, and on a phone **two
  on one glass** — each half of the screen wakes its own stick (a tap in your half darts, a flick
  lances, a held second finger swells). **Together** is a co-op dive where touching shares mass between
  you, and from the second depth some chambers carry **a door for two**: a warm pad and a cool one, as
  far apart as the chamber allows, joined by a faint thread — the way on wears their seal until both
  are held at once for a breath (the thread lights from each end that is held, then fills). **Versus** is a match of five little games, first to two: **Rain** (light rains into the dish;
  the larger membrane drinks the smaller), **Crown** (hold the crown alone and it fills with your
  colour — but it is shy: held three seconds straight, or fought over for two, it hops across the dish
  and the chase begins again), **Spark** (a touch passes it on; it burns, so whoever holds it runs hot;
  do not be holding it when time runs out) and **Race** (three courses — the Weir, the Gauntlet, the
  Slings: touch the far light, then first one home) and **Masks** — ten strangers and the two of you,
  everyone in the same moonlit opal, the same size, the same walk: dart into the other one, or eat four
  lights unseen. Nothing on the glass says which one is you (no stick ring, no pointer feelers, no sound
  of your own — the counts are a secret), so you find yourself by moving, and hide by moving as they
  move: they stroll somewhere, stop to look about, drift toward a light they happen to be near, start
  at nothing now and then. Every light eaten becomes a little moon circling whoever ate it — a glutton,
  or someone one light from winning. Dart into a stranger and you are unmasked for three seconds; when
  the round ends the masks fall and the strangers are only glass. On one keyboard Ember may take the
  arrows and Enter, so the pointer does not give them away. A card between rounds keeps the score, says
  how the round was won, and carries the round's long exposure — both ways woven over the dish, Ember's
  warm and Tide's cool. Alone, play
  **Versus a neighbour**: the machine plays Tide, with a player's verbs, a player's speed and a
  player's moment of reaction (in Masks it wears the strangers' walk and judges only what you could
  see — who ate, who darted at someone, who moved like nobody else — and it is wrong sometimes); the
  door keeps your record against it. Masks also has a door of its own: **two of you**, or **alone** — a
  match of finding the machine among ten strangers. `?mode=duet&ai&bot` lets the machine play both sides.
- **Link** — the same two modes on **two devices, with no server**. One hosts, one joins; they find each
  other by handing over two lines of text (any messenger). The host's picture and sound stream to the
  guest, whose pointer or finger is player two (move · tap to dart, hold to lance · second finger to
  swell). No ICE server is named, so nothing is sent to anyone else — and so the two devices must be
  able to reach each other directly (the same network).
- **Pool** — a sandbox with nothing to win. **Every finger holds a pinch of gel**: pull a strand between
  two fingers, pinch it thin, tear it — let go and it gathers itself (the mouse does the same with one
  hand). Blow **bubbles**: gel that touches one swallows it and swells with the air until a dart
  breathes it out again. Give the pool a **down** — and with motion sensing on, tip the device to
  pour. Pour oil, quicksilver,
  resin, foam and ferrofluid, drop gears, blades, wells, poles, turning rings, sling bands, geysers,
  sponges, mimics, currents, vents and frost, bathe to become iron, quicksilver, armour, foam or
  spore, invite neighbours, and watch one physics answer everything. Or keep a terrarium: release a
  school, a cloud of fireflies, shy sparks, drift, a seed pod that grows back.
- **Workshop** — the Pool's door asks: knead, make, or play what someone made. On the **bench** you
  build a chamber of your own. Drag to paint light (each new light a step up the tune). Draw walls in
  six surfaces — **stone** the gel can grip and climb, **glass** nothing holds to, **resin** that
  strings, **grit** that shears, a **membrane** that throws back, **ice** that chills — or tap a pen
  for a round stone. Set down **switches and doors** (a door opens when every switch is held at once;
  in a room with no switch, when the way out wakes), **lanterns** (every one lit before the way out
  will open), and any machine, fluid, neighbour or living light of the Pool; drag to aim a band, a
  current, a watcher, to stretch a film or a door across a passage. Choose the **plate** (its light
  and its music) and, if you like, give the chamber a **down** — then it plays like a little physics
  platformer: darts are leaps, stone ledges are climbable, a band aimed up is a trampoline, and an open
  way out lifts you into it. Everything set down lives at once; the sleeper waits where it will wake;
  walls stand as wet glass until the chamber is played, when they are set into the plate. **Play it
  through once and it is sealed**: your run becomes its record (Swift means beating it), and *Copy*
  turns the chamber — record and all — into one line of text. Whoever pastes it into *Play a chamber
  someone made* gets the same room in any window shape (it turns over its diagonal), with a generated
  name of its own and your run racing them as a blue bead; beat it, and the line they copy on carries
  *their* run instead. A link works too: `index.html?chamber=<line>`. The chambers you are handed are
  kept, to play again — beside four that someone left on the bench before you (switches and a door; a
  room with a down; a river and its lanterns; a watcher's gallery) — no record on them yet: your
  first run through is the one you race. On the bench, ✥ moves anything (the sleeper and the way out included), ⌫
  erases with a tap or a sweep, ↶ undoes, and ◫ **mirrors**: whatever you set down, paint or draw
  appears again across the middle — a wheel's reflection turns the other way, so two blades set down
  once close like shears. A chamber of your own, sealed, keeps a picture in the Album.

Anything that parts you — a comb, a blade, a pole's throw — makes **siblings**, not debris. A real
share of you (fourteen grains or more) is you: steerable (click it / Tab), callable (rally), and the
open pore calls all of you home as you settle onto it — and a called sibling **finds its way**: round
walls, through open gates and through membranes it is small enough for, instead of leaning on the
nearest wall. A mere splash hesitates a moment and rolls
home by itself — as beads of quicksilver or iron always do — and if you are ever left steering a
speck while the bulk of you lies elsewhere, the will moves to the bulk.

If you are ever reduced to almost nothing you become an **ember**: nothing can drink you, you move
faster, every mote counts double until you are whole again — and if the room has no light left to
give, the slide itself weeps a little near you (as it does, more slowly, whenever you have been worn
well below your beginnings while something bigger still hunts the room). There are no dead ends.

## How it works

Everything is drawn on the CPU, on purpose: the game was built and play-tested on a machine with no
GPU behind a remote desktop, so it is designed to cost only what moves.

- **Matter** (`src/sim`) — a viscoelastic particle continuum: double-density relaxation (surface
  tension that never switches off — a two-particle droplet is still a pearl), plastic springs with
  yield and creep, per-material rheology, immiscible phases, blade severing, connected-component
  splitting and contextual re-fusion, signed-distance walls with slick tangential / tacky normal
  contact, kinematic machines, force zones. Bodies **breathe**: every grain's rest density swells and
  ebbs with a slow wave travelling round the body (angular modes 2 and 3 — area is moved, never made),
  serene at rest, taut when startled, held when veiled. Shear-thickening armour flows at a walk and
  takes a blow as one piece. A launched body glides, skims currents, and can be bent but not braked.
  A current acts on bodies, not on loose grains: a cohesive body shares most of the drag across its
  whole self, a torrent becomes the frame its will swims in, and nothing keeps its grip in one — so a
  river carries you away whole instead of shearing off whatever dipped a toe in.
- **Will** (`src/life`) — local actuation with global viscoelastic coupling; a crown of verlet feelers
  that trail, reach, fetch and startle; colonies of siblings with focus and rally; small legible
  brains for the other species (they judge you by silhouette, so a held breath can bluff them).
- **Playtesting** (`src/core/bot.js`, `tools/`) — a path-planning Bot uses the same verbs through the
  same input record as a player. It finishes all twenty-eight chambers in landscape and portrait, sets
  the par times, plays Bloom, proves which daily sprints are fair, and powers `?bot`. It is not a
  person: `tools/browser/hands.mjs` lets whoever is developing play one decision at a time with their
  eyes on real frames — which is how the ghost lights, the dragged relay, the shredding river and the
  re-sealing film were found. `stranger.mjs` plays as someone told nothing; `senses.mjs` measures what
  cannot be heard or felt from a silent machine.
- **Light** (`src/render`) — each room's backdrop is painted once, per pixel, like a photographic
  plate: a pool of stage light, a caustic network on the water film, bevelled frosted-glass walls with
  bubbles frozen inside, an etched grid, shadows, a reflectance map — and a garden of diatoms,
  radiolaria, fronds and calibration reticles (`src/world/decor.js`) baked in as decals. Per frame only
  dirty tiles are restored and re-uploaded. Gel is an implicit surface: particles (and the crown's
  tapering arms) splat a field → a crease-free dome → normals → refraction with dispersion,
  Beer–Lambert absorption, in-scatter shaped by a slope-indexed *studio* (a soft window, a kicker, a
  sky rim, and the bright crescent where a dome focuses light on its far side — highlights are
  reflections, never painted), and a photon-splat lightmap that gives every body a true shadow and a
  focused caustic. Moving machines are stamped by the browser's own rasteriser. An internal pixel
  budget is measured at start-up and re-tuned between chambers; long frames fall back to 30 fps pacing
  instead of slowing time.
- **Sound** (`src/core/audio.js`) — fully procedural WebAudio, and a score that listens. Every place
  has its own piece — a key, a pulse, a slow progression (Dew in E, the Glassworks in B dorian,
  Chemistry in F lydian with one chromatic step aside, the Abyss in C minor; wardens turn their
  stratum's key minor and walk the bass on every beat). A pad glides between voicings, the bass
  breathes once a bar (twice, like a heart, when something near can hurt you), and a glass layer sings
  chord tones as fast as the creature lives — stillness thins it to a few chimes. Every tonal effect
  is drawn from the chord sounding *now*, so a chain of motes is an arpeggio of the harmony under it
  and a firefly's flash is in tune. An open pore calls with a three-note motif from its own side of the
  room (louder as you near, a fourth note when you are almost there, and it does not nag); leaving
  resolves the music home. Left alone, the creature hums two notes of the chord on the beat; fireflies
  retune their clocks until they flash on the downbeat; the pause menu sends the music behind the glass. The body
  has a continuous voice too: a silk whisper when it glides against things, a hum that climbs as it is
  stretched or coiled, a low pulse when something near can hurt it.
